# Account portal

**Routes:** `/account` (Overview), `/account/sponsorship`, `/account/payments`, `/account/team`, `/account/settings`, and `/sponsor/start` (outside the portal)
**Primary files:** `client/src/account-portal/`, `client/src/billing/`, `client/src/sponsorship/`, `convex/organizations.ts`, `convex/billingCommands.ts`, `convex/sponsorships.ts`

The portal belongs to the signed-in person. Every signed-in user on the web build has an account page; the organization's content appears only for someone with a role in an organization. It is a route group inside the dashboard app, not a separate app. The shared components in `client/src/billing/` render both the organization's subscription and payments and, for a person's own sponsorship.

## Where it runs

- On the dashboard origin, so it shares the dashboard's Convex Auth session (kept in `localStorage`, per origin). `woofx3.tv/account/*` is routed there by the edge.
- Outside `BroadcastShell` and `OnboardingGuard`, with its own slim layout (`AccountPortal.tsx`): it needs no engine, only a signed-in user. Someone signed out goes through sign-in and back to the same `/account/…` path (`AuthGuard`'s `?next=`, clamped by `safeNextPath`).
- The marketing site's "Start free trial" link opens `/account?start=trial`. Someone who owns no organization with an engine is sent straight on to onboarding, where choosing a hosted engine starts the trial (`start-trial.ts`); an owner who already has an engine sees the Overview.
- **Web build only.** `App.tsx` imports it behind `IS_DESKTOP`, so the desktop build carries no `/account` route, and the same holds for `/sponsor/start`; `account-portal/boundary.test.ts` holds both.
- The routes are `ACCOUNT_PORTAL_ROUTE` in `App.tsx`. Nothing redirects from other paths; a link must name `/account` or one of the tabs.

## Data

- `organizations.mine` lists the caller's organizations, each with their role, a `can` map from `orgCan`, its engines, the subscription and seats. What a role may not see comes back empty: members get no subscription and no seats, and only the engines they're on. Only the owner gets the active discount.
- `organizations.members` (owner and admin) lists members with their engines. Email addresses are the owner's only, masked until shown, as on the Team page. Roles change and members are removed through `accountMembers.updateMemberRole` / `removeMember`. Invitations are sent from the portal's Team page and, for the selected engine, the engine's own Team page.
- `organizations.rename` (owner only). Engines are renamed with `instances.update`, also owner only (`renameEngine`); the address, `url`, is owner only too (`changeEngineUrl`), is refused for hosted engines, must be a public https address, and drops the engine's stored credentials when it changes, so the engine has to register again.
- Billing actions are the owner's (`manageBilling`) and live in `convex/billingCommands.ts`: `startCheckout`, `previewPromotionCode`, `openPortal`, `invoices` (answers null to anyone else) and `checkoutState` (where a checkout stands, for the page the customer returns to). Each asks the management plane through `convex/lib/billingClient.ts`, server-side and only for something a person just did; the plane reads invoices live and woofx3 stores none. The client authenticates with `MAINTENANCE_API_URL` and two keys bound to a billing owner in the backoffice, each with only the `billing:read` and `billing:write` scopes: `MAINTENANCE_BILLING_ORG_KEY` (owner `org`) for organization routes (`/v1/billing/accounts/*`, promotion codes and checkout state by `accountId`) and `MAINTENANCE_BILLING_USER_KEY` (owner `user`) for sponsorship routes (`/v1/billing/users/*`, sponsorship terms and checkout state by `userId`). Each client method names its owner, and the plane answers 403 `forbidden_billing_owner` to a key on the other owner's route. Setting only one key is a startup error for billing; while neither is set, `MAINTENANCE_API_KEY` serves both owners and a warning is logged once. A checkout's `Idempotency-Key` derives from the org, the terms and a 30-minute window, so a retry or double click opens one checkout.
- A new org asks the plane to `ensure` its billing (`billingCommands.ensureBilling`, scheduled): a failure never blocks creating the org; Convex retries it with capped, jittered backoff for about a day, then logs an error.
- Convex holds no payment code and no billing lifecycle. The management plane owns subscriptions, trials, exemptions, discounts and the provider's webhooks, and pushes each org's billing to Convex as a projection (`orgBilling`, one row per org, written by `POST /api/ops/accounts/:id/billing-projection`; see [Ops API](../patterns/ops-api.md)). `organizations.mine`, the access gates and the entitlements all read it through `billingFor` (`convex/org/billing.ts`) and never call the plane, so they keep working while it is down. An org with no projection row yet behaves as one with no subscription: nothing is refused, and its entitlements are the code defaults with no plan.
- The billing banner (`convex/billingBanner.ts`) is computed from the projection: the last week of a trial, and while a payment is overdue. Billing emails and their schedule belong to the plane; Convex sends none.

## Who sees what

`portal-pages.ts` maps each tab to the scopes that list it (`scopes`) and the org action it needs (`visibleWith`; null means no action). A tab the viewer cannot see sends them to `/account`.

| Scope | Viewer | Tabs |
|---|---|---|
| Personal | Anyone signed in | Overview, Sponsorship, Your settings |
| Organization | Owner | Overview, Engines, Payments, Team, Upgrade |
| Organization | Admin | Overview, Engines, Team |
| Organization | Member | Overview, Engines |

Upgrade (the Plans page) is docked at the foot of the nav, above the collapse control (`docked` on the nav item).

The header's switcher (`portal-scope.ts`, `ScopeSwitcher`) chooses what the portal shows: **Personal**, which everyone has, or one of the viewer's organizations. Personal holds a person's own things: sponsorships (with their receipts), their settings, and the Overview that lists every engine they can open across their organizations. An organization's scope holds that organization's dashboard, payments, team and settings; sponsorship and personal settings are not offered there. A person with no organization sees Personal alone. The portal opens on the organization of the engine they were last in, else one they own, else the first, else Personal.

| Tab | Path | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Overview, personal | `/account` | every engine the viewer can open, with "Launch dashboard", or a large call to action to create one |
| Overview, organization | `/account` | a dashboard (`organization-dashboard.tsx`): the plan and where it stands, the next billing date (the trial's end while trialing), the engine and seat counts, a notice when billing needs attention, the engines (hosting, build state, an "Upgrade available" badge when a newer engine release is on offer, "Launch dashboard", "Manage"; the owner creates), and the owner renames the organization from the page header, and the last five invoices for the billing owner |
| Engines | `/account/engines` | every engine of the organization as a card (hosting, build state, release and an "Upgrade available" badge, "Launch dashboard", "Manage"); the owner creates more |
| Engine | `/account/engines/:instanceId` | one engine's dashboard (`engine-detail.tsx`): health (`engine-health.ts`, plus a live connection check), release and whether an upgrade is on offer, the people who can open it with their role, its limits (storage, module storage, the organization's seats), the modules (a count, those not running or failed first with their message, a scrolling list with a search once there are more than eight, and nothing marked on a module that is running) and linked streaming accounts. The engine cards on the Engines page and the Overview show the same reading in short: a health dot and label, when the engine last reported in, and the linked channel. The owner renames it here. An id outside the selected organization goes back to Engines |
| Sponsorship | `/account/sponsorship` | the viewer's sponsorships and the receipts of what they paid |
| Payments | `/account/payments` | (`manageBilling`) the organization's invoices |
| Team | `/account/team` | (`manageMembers`) one list of the organization's people, each with the engines they can open and their role on each. A plain member's engines are added (`engineMembers.add`), changed (`setRole`) or taken away (`remove`) per engine; owners and admins have every engine. The organization role changes and a member is removed through `accountMembers`. "Invite to an engine" opens the invite form for the chosen engine, and the pending invitations of every engine (`invitations.listForOrganization`) can be revoked |
| Upgrade | `/account/organization/plans` | (`manageBilling`) the plan the organization is on and every plan, the current one marked: choose a plan and check out; manage billing |

An invitation names an engine. Accepting it makes the person a member of the organization with access to that engine only; they see no other engine until it is granted.

An admin sees no organization billing: `organizations.mine` sends the subscription only to a role with `manageBilling`, the owner.  The pages work with no organization at all: `organizations.mine` answers an empty list, the switcher shows only Personal, and no organization query runs.

The Overview lists the organization's engines as cards, each with how it is hosted, where its build stands and a "Launch dashboard" button; members see the ones they can open. With no engine it shows a large call to action: the owner creates one here, a viewer with no organization goes to onboarding, and anyone else is told to ask an owner. An organization's first engine comes from onboarding; the owner (`createEngine`) adds more with "Create another engine" (`new-engine-dialog.tsx`):

- **Hosted by woofx3**: a name and an address (the slug, suggested from the name and checked as it is typed with `provisioning.checkSlug`). `provisioning.startNewEngine` makes the instance in the organization through `provisioningInternal.prepareNewEngine`, which gives the owner and the org's admins their engine roles (`grantNewEngineMembers`) and marks it a first run so setup seeds it, then asks the maintenance API for the engine the way the first engine is asked for (`requestEngine`): the same trial `ensure`, the same refusal while the organization is suspended, and the same idempotency key until the outcome is known. Asking again while the engine is being built returns the same engine. Unlike the first engine, it gets no Twitch channel up front; its own setup connects one. A taken address answers `slug_refused`, and the next attempt reuses the same instance. Only one engine of an organization is built at a time.
- **I run it myself**: a name and a server address, created with `instances.create` and registered with `registration.registerInstance`, as onboarding's own-engine form does. It asks for no subscription.

Either way the new engine becomes the selected one (`$currentInstanceId`) and the dashboard goes to `/setup`, where a hosted engine shows its build progress and Try again (`provisioning.retry`) like the first engine's. Nothing changes the owner's default engine, so signing in still opens the one it did. Plans have no engine-count limit, and seats stay per organization whatever the number of engines.

The payment provider returns organization checkouts to `/account`; the Overview sends the billing owner on to Plans with the same query, where the result is shown. Plans shows an "Active plan" card (the plan's name, where it stands, what it includes) and below it every plan, the one the organization pays for marked "Current plan"; while a provider subscription is running a plan's button changes the plan in place (below), and "Manage billing" still opens the provider's portal for the card. An organization with only self-hosted engines, or a complimentary one, sees the plans with no buttons. The Plans page picks its content in `billing-view.ts`: an organization with only self-hosted engines reads "No subscription needed for self-hosted engines"; an exempt one shows its plan with no buttons; a running provider subscription offers "Manage billing" (the provider's portal); otherwise the owner chooses a plan and checks out. A checkout is refused while the provider subscription has not ended: one in good standing answers `already_subscribed`, and one whose payments are failing (past due, or suspended with the subscription still alive) answers `subscription_exists`, which sends the owner to the provider's portal to fix the payment method instead of paying for a second subscription. List prices in `@/billing/format.ts` must match the provider's prices and the marketing pricing page.

### Changing plan in place

For an organization with a running provider subscription, choosing another plan or interval opens `plan-change-dialog.tsx`, which asks the plane what the move would charge (`billingCommands.previewPlanChange`) before anything changes. A move up (a higher plan, or a longer interval of the same plan) is charged now: the dialog lists the credit for the unused time and the charge for the rest of the period, the total due today, and what is charged from the next period on. A move down charges nothing: the organization keeps its plan until the end of the paid period and the dialog says when the new one starts. Confirming calls `billingCommands.changePlan`; a refused card is `payment_declined` and nothing changes. A move down stays booked at the plane, so the Plans page reads it (`billingCommands.scheduledPlanChange`) and shows it with a "Keep my plan" button, which asks for the plan the organization is already on. After a move up the page says the plan is updating until the plane's projection reaches `organizations.mine`. Organizations still on their trial, or with no paid subscription, go through checkout as before.

## Sponsorships

A sponsorship belongs to a person, not an organization. The management plane owns it and its payments; Convex stores nothing and asks the plane for each read. `convex/sponsorships.ts` has the actions: `terms`, `mine`, `start`, `changeAmount`, `cancel`, `paymentMethodSession` and `checkoutState`. Each requires a signed-in viewer and passes the viewer's own user id to the plane; no action takes a user id as an argument. They use the typed commands in `convex/lib/billingClient.ts` and the same error mapping and credential as the billing actions. `start` builds its return address from `SITE_URL` and an `Idempotency-Key` from the viewer, amount, interval and a 30-minute window; the client supplies neither.

- **`/sponsor/start`** (`client/src/sponsorship/SponsorStart.tsx`) sits outside the broadcast shell, behind `AuthGuard`, so a signed-out visitor signs in or registers and returns with the query intact. `amountCents` and `interval` in the address only prefill the form (`prefillFromSearch`); a missing or malformed value falls back to $250 monthly, and the amount is validated against `sponsorships.terms` before "Continue to payment" calls `sponsorships.start` and redirects to the returned `url`.
- **Sponsorship** (`sponsorship-section.tsx`) lists the viewer's sponsorships, newest first, with a summary and a notice for a failed payment or a scheduled end. A monthly sponsorship that is active or past due offers "Change amount", "Update payment method" (a provider redirect, `ProviderRedirectButton`) and "Cancel sponsorship" (`ConfirmDialog`); a cancel always ends at the end of the paid period, and the action takes no way to ask otherwise. A one-time sponsorship, or one already set to end, offers none of them. With no sponsorship the section is a short pitch and a "Become a sponsor" link.
- **Overview**, for a viewer with no organization, shows the create-engine call to action linking to `/auth/onboarding`. The dashboard sends such a viewer here rather than to onboarding when they have no engine (see [Auth and onboarding](/ui/auth-onboarding#setup)).
- **Payments** lists the organization's plan invoices, newest first (`payment-rows.ts`), for the billing owner (`manageBilling`). An empty history shows only once the lookup has answered; a failed lookup is one message. An organization with no billing yet (the plane answers `subscription_not_found`) has no invoices, not an error. Sponsor payments are on the Sponsorship page. The list stacks each payment below `sm`, and its link column exists only while some payment has a link.
- **Checkout return.** The provider returns to `/account/sponsorship?sponsorship=done&sponsorshipCheckout=<id>` or `/account/sponsorship?sponsorship=cancelled`; the keys are not the organization checkout's `checkout` / `checkoutId`, so the two never read each other's. On `done` the section polls `sponsorships.checkoutState` with `useCheckoutPoll` and the `CHECKOUT_POLL` bounds, shows "Finalizing your payment..." until the plane reports `complete` (the provider's webhook is what completes it), then reloads the list. The page never claims success on its own, and on timeout it says the payment is still processing. Returning from the card-update page (`?sponsorshipPaymentMethod=returned`) shows "Payment method updated." once and removes the key from the address. A sponsorship set to end shows "Ending" followed by the end date as its state.
- Sponsorship wording (states, amounts, notices) lives in `client/src/sponsorship/`, not in `client/src/billing/`.

## Entry points

The profile menu's "Account" entry (any signed-in user, web build only) opens `/account`. The Team page has a "Manage organization" button for org owners and admins on a hosted engine, in the web build. Billing banners and in-app notices link to `/account`.
